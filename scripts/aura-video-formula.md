# The Aura video formula

Every Aura video script follows this. Written by Senami, 19 Nov 2026, from the owner's rules:
- hook first;
- sell outcomes, not features;
- say it plainly;
- show the real app.

## The one-line version

**Ask for the dream → name the wall → show Aura breaking it → land the win → tell them what to do.**

## The five beats (default 25 seconds)

| Beat | Time | What it does | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. HOOK | 0–3s | Names the outcome the viewer wants, as a yes/no question. | Spoken and on screen from the first frame, with sound from frame one. Use their words, not ours. One outcome only. |
| 2. THE WALL | 3–8s | The real thing stopping them. | One problem, said plainly. A Nigerian reality they live, e.g. no money for lessons, no light, a lecturer who goes too fast, a 400-page textbook. |
| 3. THE DEMO | 8–20s | Real Aura clips breaking that exact wall. | 2–3 clips at most, each one action then its result. The narration says what they do and what they get. The demo must answer the wall from beat 2. |
| 4. THE WIN | 20–23s | The outcome again, now within reach. | Show life after: ready for the exam hall, the first-class letter, the plane ticket, the meeting going well. |
| 5. NEXT STEP | 23–25s | What to do now. | "Start preparing for your [outcome] with Aura today" plus the real price. Never "free". |

**Story version (45–60s):** the same five beats, carried by a character (like Kemi). Beat 2 becomes the character's struggle, beat 4 the character's result. Label it "Dramatisation" when the character reaches a specific score or result.

## Hook formula

**"Do you want to [outcome], but [wall]?"**

- JAMB: "Do you want to score 300 and above in JAMB, but you don't know where to start?"
- WAEC: "Do you want A's in WAEC, but nobody explains the questions you get wrong?"
- Parents: "Do you want your child to get into university this year, but lesson fees are too much?"
- University: "Do you want to graduate with a first class, but you leave lectures with notes you can't understand?"
- IELTS (once priced): "Do you want to pass IELTS and travel abroad, but you're not sure what band you'd get today?"
- Professionals: "Do you want that promotion, but you never have time to read the reports?"

Short form when the wall comes in beat 2: "Do you want to score 300 in JAMB? Let me show you how."

Never use:
- riddles or clever lines;
- "Watch this";
- numbers puzzles;
- a hook about the app.

## The promise rule

The outcome is the viewer's goal and Aura helps them reach it. Say "help you score 300", "get you ready", "start preparing for your first class". Never "Aura will make you score 300" or "guaranteed".

## Demo clip library (beat 3)

Match the wall to the clip. Every clip is a real screen recording of the live app.

| Wall | Demo clip | Narration pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Nobody to explain the questions I get wrong | Practice question → wrong answer → quick lesson opens → **Listen to the lesson** (words light up, speed control) | "When you get one wrong, Aura teaches you the topic. Tap Listen and it reads it to you while the words light up." |
| I understand better when I see it | **Watch it as a video**: the animated lesson for that topic | "Still not clear? Watch it as a video lesson, step by step." |
| My notes/handout don't make sense | Upload or snap notes → **create a video lesson** from them | "Snap your notes, and Aura turns them into a video lesson you can watch." |
| Too much to read, no time | Snap or upload a handout → **listen** (on the bus, in the dark) | "Snap the handout and listen to it on your way home." |
| Exam hall panic, time runs out | **Timed CBT mock**, timer counting down, score at the end | "Every week, sit a full timed mock, just like the real CBT." |
| Don't know where to start | Exam home → subject → practice by topic / study plan | "Pick your subject, and Aura shows you what to practise first." |
| Lecturer goes too fast | **Record** the lecture → transcript → key points | "Record the lecture, and Aura writes down every word and pulls out the key points." |
| Can't find the answer in my textbook | **Ask your handout** → answer with the page it came from | "Ask your handout a question, and Aura answers and shows you the page." |
| Parents can't see progress / have to pay | **Parent report** link and **parent-pay** link | "Your parents can see how you're doing every week, and pay with a link you send them." |
| IELTS essay (once priced) | Write an essay → marked with a band and fixes | "Write your essay, and Aura marks it and shows you what to fix." |
| 80-page report before a meeting | Upload report → summary → listen in traffic | "Upload the report and listen to the summary in traffic." |

Don't show:
- lessons heard in Yoruba, Hausa or Igbo (removed);
- Memorize;
- auto top-up;
- anything still marked "coming soon".

## Production rules

- 9:16, 1080×1920, captions burned in, text clear of the bottom 25% and the right edge.
- Sound and voice from the first frame. No silent openings, no logo intros.
- Default 25 seconds; story films 45–60 seconds; never longer.
- The owner's chosen hook visual: an animated problem metaphor in the first seconds (e.g. notes falling apart), with a face or mascot circle.
- Full conversational sentences ("So if…", "That's why…"). Pidgin only in character lines, always subtitled.
- Characters are scenarios, never presented as real users or testimonials.
- **Real people first.** Real actors or the owner on camera, plus real app recordings, carry the trust. Use AI for the hook's problem-metaphor animation, scenery and b-roll. If a person on screen is AI-made, label the post as AI. Research summary in chat, 19 Nov 2026: viewers spot full-AI ads, find them less engaging and remember them less; AI-made people hurt trust more than AI-made scenery.
- Price is always the real one: Exam/Campus pass from ₦1,000 a week, Pro ₦5,000 a month.

## Pre-approval checklist

- [ ] The first spoken words are an outcome question, and the same words are on screen
- [ ] One outcome, one wall, and the demo answers that wall
- [ ] Every demo clip is the real, current app
- [ ] No guarantee wording; "Dramatisation" label if a character gets a specific result
- [ ] No "free", no brackets or placeholders, real price
- [ ] 25s (or 45–60s story), sound from frame one
- [ ] Owner has approved before anything is posted
